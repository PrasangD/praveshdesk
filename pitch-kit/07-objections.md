# The nine things you will be asked

Answer briefly, without defensiveness, and get back to the job. An answer that takes two minutes
sounds rehearsed; one that takes fifteen seconds sounds true.

---

### 1. "You have a full-time job. How do I know this gets finished?"

The honest one, and the one you must never be rattled by. It is asked by serious buyers.

> "Fair question. That's exactly why I take two builds at a time and give a date before I start —
> if I took five I'd miss all of them. You pay half up front and the rest only after it's run for
> a week on your data, so if I don't deliver, you're out half of a small number. And everything
> is handed over with the code, so you're never stuck waiting for me."

Never apologise for it. The capacity limit is a feature and your pricing page already says so.

---

### 2. "Can't we just use [Zapier / Power Automate / an off-the-shelf tool]?"

> "Sometimes, genuinely. If your job fits one of those, I'll tell you in the audit and you should
> use it — it'll be cheaper than me. Where they fall down is when the rules are specific to how
> you work, when the data needs real cleaning, or when you need it to say *why* it rejected
> something. That's most of the jobs I get asked about, but not all of them."

---

### 3. "What if you disappear?"

> "Then it keeps running. It's on your accounts, not mine, and at handover you get the code, the
> credentials and written notes. That's the actual test of whether something was built properly.
> Any developer can pick it up — it's deliberately boring code."

---

### 4. "₹9,999 just to look at it?"

> "I'd rather charge for the half day than pad the build price to cover it. And it comes off the
> first build in full, so if you go ahead it costs you nothing. If the report says nothing's worth
> automating, you've saved a lot more than ₹9,999 and you keep the report."

If they still resist and they are otherwise a good fit, use a free-audit place — but *name* it as
one of the five, so it reads as an opening offer and not as a discount.

---

### 5. "Our data is confidential."

> "Understood. I sign an NDA before any audit that touches real data — yours if you have one. I
> work on samples with the sensitive parts removed wherever that's enough, I take the least access
> that does the job, and everything runs in your accounts. You can revoke my access at any time."

---

### 6. "Send me a quote and I'll have a look."

They mean: I do not want to spend time on this. Do not send a blind quote.

> "I can, but it'd be a guess, and guesses come out high because I'd be pricing the risk. Fifteen
> minutes on a call and I can give you a real number instead. If it's not worth fifteen minutes,
> it's probably not the right job to start with — which is also a useful answer."

---

### 7. "This will put people out of work."

Do not dodge it. Your website already takes a position.

> "I won't build something designed to do that quietly — it's on the list of things I don't build.
> What usually happens is somebody gets a day a week back and spends it on work they were already
> behind on. If your plan is headcount, that's your decision to make openly, not something to
> outsource to a script."

---

### 8. "Can you do it cheaper?"

> "Not without taking something out. Tell me what matters least and I'll re-scope it — I'd rather
> build a smaller thing properly than a big thing badly."

Then actually re-scope. Never just drop the number.

---

### 9. "We'll think about it."

> "Of course. Can I ask what you'd be weighing up — the money, the timing, or whether it's the
> right job to start with?"

Then take the answer at face value, agree a date to speak again, and leave. If they say no later,
reply thanking them and send the calculator link. A gracious no is worth more in this market than
a reluctant yes, because the town is small and people talk.

---

## Two answers to have ready that nobody asks for

**"What's the catch?"** — "I'm one person, so I'm slower than an agency and I turn down work I
can't do properly. If you need twenty people next month, I'm the wrong call."

**"Who else have you done this for?"** — Before you have references: *"You'd be among the first
five, which is why the audit is free and the build is at the bottom of its band. In return I'd
ask to write up what was built and what it saved, with your name on it only if you're happy."*
Say it plainly. Pretending to have clients you do not have is the one lie that ends this business.
