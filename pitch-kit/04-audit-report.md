# The audit report

This is the deliverable for the ₹9,999 audit, and it is the most important document you will
write. Somebody has paid for it. It has to be worth keeping even if they never buy a build — that
is the standard, and meeting it is what makes the fixed price on the next page believable.

**Target length:** 3–5 pages. **Turnaround:** within three working days, always.

---

## Template

>
> Prepared by [YOUR NAME] · [DATE]
> Based on a visit on [DATE], with [NAMES AND ROLES]
>

>

>
> [One paragraph: which processes, who showed you, and what you were told about them. Name the
> people — it shows you spoke to the people doing the work, not only the person who hired you.]
>
> I looked at [N] processes:
>
> 1. [Process] — [who does it]
> 2. [Process] — [who does it]
> 3. [Process] — [who does it]
>
> ## 2. What each one costs today
>
> | Process | Who | Hours/week | Hours/year | Est. cost/year |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | [Process 1] | [Role] | [X] | [X×52] | ₹[figure] |
> | [Process 2] | [Role] | [X] | [X×52] | ₹[figure] |
> | **Total** | | **[X]** | **[X]** | **₹[figure]** |
>
> Costs use a loaded hourly rate of ₹[rate], based on [how you got it]. An eight-hour day and a
> twenty-two-day month. These are estimates from the figures given to me on the day, not measured
> times.
>
> [Then one paragraph naming the costs **not** in the table: errors, delays, the risk sitting with
> one person, the overtime, the thing that goes wrong when they are on leave. This paragraph is
> usually what the client quotes back to you.]
>
> ## 3. Recommendations, in the order I would do them
>
> ### 3.1 [Process] — **automate**
>
> **Today:** [What happens, concretely. Their words.]
> **Instead:** [What replaces it, in one or two sentences.]
> **Effort:** [Band] — **₹[from] to ₹[to]**, ready in [timeframe]
> **Recovers:** roughly [X] hours a year, about ₹[figure]
> **Pays for itself in:** about [N] months
> **Depends on:** [Access, an export, a decision they need to make, a person's time]
> **Risk:** [The thing most likely to make this harder than it looks. Always name one.]
>
> ### 3.2 [Process] — **fix without code**
>
> **Today:** [What happens.]
> **Instead:** [The setting, the formula, the template, the habit. Be specific enough to act on.]
> **Cost:** nothing, about [N] hours of somebody's time
> **Recovers:** roughly [X] hours a year
>
> *Include at least one of these if one exists. It is the most credible page in the report.*
>
> ### 3.3 [Process] — **do not automate**
>
> **Today:** [What happens.]
> **Why not:** [It needs judgement / it is twice a year / the process is changing / the data would
> have to be entered twice / it is genuinely cheaper by hand.]
>
> ## 4. If you did all of it
>
> | | |
> | --- | --- |
> | Hours recovered a year | [X] |
> | Estimated value | ₹[figure] |
> | Total to build | ₹[figure] |
> | Payback | about [N] months |

> Assuming about 70% of each job actually goes away. Almost nothing is automated to zero — there
> is always a person checking exceptions, and the estimate should say so.
>
> ## 5. What I would do first, and why
>
> [One short paragraph. Name **one** thing. If they only ever do one, this is it — usually the
> best ratio of payback to risk, not the biggest number.]
>

>
> - [Access to X]
> - [A decision about Y]
> - [About N hours of [person]'s time during the build]

## Rules for writing it

**Always include a "fix without code" item if one exists.** It costs you a small sale and buys
you the client's trust permanently. It is also usually true — a surprising share of manual work
is a missing filter, a wrong default, or a meeting that should be an email.

**Always include something you would not automate.** A report that recommends automating
everything reads as a sales document, because it is one.

**Always name a risk for each build.** "The export has no invoice number in it, so we'd need to
match on date and amount — that's doable but it's where the estimate could move." Naming the risk
before you are paid is what makes the fixed price credible afterwards.

**Use their words for the processes.** If they call it "the MIS", call it the MIS. Do not rename
their work into vendor language.

**Give the ranges honestly.** If you do not know enough to quote tightly, say what you would need
to know to tighten it. A wide range with a stated reason beats a narrow one you will regret.

**Send it within three working days.** The report arriving when you said it would is the first
evidence they have that your dates mean anything.
