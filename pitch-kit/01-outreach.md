# Outreach

## The principle

You are not selling automation. You are naming a specific job you believe somebody at their
company hates, and offering to look at it for free. Nobody replies to "I can help you automate
your processes." People reply to "who rebuilds that report every Monday?"

So: **one job, named, in the first line.** If you cannot name one, you have not researched enough
to send the message yet.

---

## Cold email — operations or finance lead

> **Subject:** the report somebody rebuilds every Monday
>
> Hi [NAME],
>
> I'm a DevOps engineer in Dombivli. I automate the jobs companies still do by hand — the weekly
> report that gets assembled from four places, the reconciliation between two systems, the
> documents somebody generates one at a time.
>
> I don't know which of those is your version. But most companies your size have one, and the
> person doing it usually loses a day a week to it.
>
> If you tell me the job, I'll tell you honestly whether it's worth automating — including "no,
> just fix the spreadsheet" when that's the answer. That conversation is free and takes fifteen
> minutes.
>
> There's a working demo here if you'd rather see it than read about it: [SITE]/demo
>
> [YOUR NAME]
> [PHONE] · [EMAIL]

**Why it works:** names three concrete jobs, admits it does not know theirs, offers a free answer
including a negative one, and links to something that runs rather than something that describes.

>
> That's what I build. Fixed price per job, on your own systems, and you keep the code. If you

## WhatsApp — only after you have met or been introduced

Never cold. It reads as spam and it is the thing this business says it does not do.

> Hi [NAME], [YOUR NAME] here — we spoke at [WHERE]. You mentioned [THE JOB THEY MENTIONED]. I
> had a look and I think it's a straightforward one. Want me to send what it'd involve? No
> obligation.

## The follow-up sequence

Two follow-ups, then stop. Anything more damages the reputation you are trying to build.

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Day 0 | The first message |
| Day 4 | Reply in the same thread with **one new specific thing** — not "just following up" |
| Day 12 | The close-the-loop message |
| After that | Nothing. Connect on LinkedIn and let the website do the work |

**Day 4 — add something, do not nudge:**

> One example, in case it makes it concrete: a four-branch daily sheet, consolidated and sent as
> one report every Monday. About three hours a week by hand, and it now takes a few milliseconds
> — including flagging the rows that need a person. Working version here: [SITE]/demo
>
> Still happy to look at yours.

**Day 12 — close the loop properly:**

> I'll leave it there — I know this isn't urgent, and I'd rather not clutter your inbox.
>
> If a job like this comes up later, I'm at [PHONE]. And if you'd rather just work out whether
> something's worth automating yourself, the calculator at [SITE]/calculator does the arithmetic
> with no form to fill in.

That last message gets replies surprisingly often, because it is the only one in their inbox that
stops.

## Where to find people around here

- **MIDC estates** — Dombivli, Ambernath, Badlapur, Taloja. Manufacturing and distribution, lots
  of parallel registers. Walking in with a printed one-pager works better here than email.
- **Wagle Estate and Ghodbunder Road, Thane** — offices and mid-size companies.
- **Airoli, Mahape, Ghansoli** — IT parks; engineering-team problems.
- **Your own network first.** The first two clients almost always come from somebody who already
  knows you are the person who automates things. Tell everyone you know what you are doing before
  you send a single cold email.
- **Local business associations and MIDC industry bodies** — one introduction is worth fifty
  emails.

## What not to do

- No bulk WhatsApp. It is on the "will not build" list on your own website; doing it yourself
  would be the fastest way to lose the argument.
- No scraped lists. Same reason.
- No "quick call?" with nothing specific in it.
- Do not discount to win the first client. Use the free-audit offer instead — it costs you a
  half-day and keeps your price list intact.
