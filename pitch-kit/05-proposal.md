# The proposal

Sent after the audit, for one specific build. Short — two pages at most. Everything in it is
either a number, a date, or a boundary.

A proposal that takes half an hour to read gets read next week. Keep it to five minutes.

## Rules

**Never send a proposal without having done the audit.** If you are quoting from a description of
the work, you are guessing, and you will either underprice it or pad it. Both cost you.

**One proposal, one job.** If they want three things, that is three prices and a suggested order,
not one large number. A big number invites a procurement process; three small ones invite a
decision.

**Put the "will not do" section in, always.** It is what makes a fixed price survivable. Every
fixed-price job that goes wrong goes wrong here.

**Give a start date that is conditional on access.** Most delays are on the client side, and this
sentence is how you say so later without it becoming an argument.

**Make the acceptance test theirs.** "You switch over when you are satisfied" costs you nothing —
you were going to run it in parallel anyway — and it removes almost all of the fear.

**30-day validity.** Not pressure; it lets you re-price later without embarrassment.
